## K. life.html — Three Defects That Only Measurement Could Have Found

`life.html` shipped with a wild mutator that counted its own failures, a nav dropdown it covered, and an evolution rule with no connection to the games. All three are recorded here because each one is invisible to a shape check, and two of them are invisible to a static checker too.

<a id="k1"></a>
### K1. The wild encounter died on arrival 60% of the time, and the HUD counted it anyway

**THE OLD POLICY CHOSE A SITE DELIBERATELY ADJACENT TO THE POPULATION.** `nextCells` was scanned for "any empty cell within 2 of a randomly chosen LIVE cell" (`life-engine.js`), so a pack was born touching the soup — median 2 adjacent live cells, up to 8 — and Conway's survival rule is `count === 2 || count === 3`. Then each pack cell was placed only `if (!nextCells.has(k))`, so a colliding site produced a **1- or 2-cell pack**, and a 2-cell pack is dead on arrival: each cell has exactly one neighbour, so both die.

Measured over 25 × 400 generations, 210 encounters, 20% seed density:

| | before |
| :--- | :--- |
| median pack life | **4 generations** (1.6 s at 1×, **0.16 s at 10×**) |
| dead within 5 generations | 59% |
| dead within 20 | 92% |
| alive at generation 400 | 4% |
| **born with fewer than 3 cells** | **36–39%** |

`spawnedCount > 0` was the success test, so `wildEncounterCount++` and `recordDiscovery()` both ran for packs that were never viable: **the HUD's 野生遭遇次數 counted attempts, not encounters.**

**THE PATTERNS WERE NEVER THE PROBLEM, AND MEASURING THAT IS WHAT MADE THE FIX OBVIOUS.** Both shipped shapes are viable alone: an L-tromino settles into a 2×2 block by generation 1 and a blinker oscillates, each holding 3 cells indefinitely on an empty grid. Two guesses about the blinker ("ends die, it collapses") were wrong, and running it took a minute.

**THE FIX IS A MOAT, NOT A SMARTER PACK.** `pickWildSite()` requires every pack cell **and a 1-cell ring around it** to be empty, re-rolls up to 500 times, and returns null otherwise — and null means **no encounter**: nothing placed, counter not advanced, no family recorded.

| policy | median life | dead ≤5 | dead ≤20 | alive @400 | partial-born |
| :--- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| before | 4 | 59% | 92% | 4% | 36–39% |
| 1-cell moat, same shapes | **24** | 5% | 19% | **57%** | **0%** |

At 10% seed density the old policy also left the grid **empty for 29 generations** (worst run 22), because the only recovery was a 2%-per-tick roll at the origin; with the moat it is **0**, since an L-tromino in genuinely empty space becomes a block. Mean population 126 → 124 at 20%, so the surviving colonies do not flood the infinite sparse grid.

**THE CANDIDATE ORIGINS ARE DE-DUPLICATED, and that is the second half of the fix.** Sampling "a random living cell, then a random offset" weights every candidate by how many living cells sit near it, so the search keeps re-finding **pockets buried inside the colony** rather than its fringe — and a pocket is where a fresh pack gets crowded out again a few generations later. Listing each candidate position once and sampling that uniformly was measured against the weighted sampler: median life 10 → 27, alive @400 32% → 57%. The anchor sample is capped at 64 so a 2,000-cell board does not build a 70,000-entry list per spawn attempt.

**Expect this shape whenever a "random placement near X" is actually a two-step sample.** The first half of the distribution — which neighbourhood you sample — is invisible in the second half — which point inside it you land on.

**Nothing consumed the event, so an encounter was invisible even when it worked.** `stats.wildSpawnEvent` was returned by `tick()` and read by **no page code at all** (`rg` across the repo found it only in the engine and in one checker), and `isWildSpawn` was never read by the renderer either. At 10× speed a wild encounter was a 0.16 s flicker and then silence — you could not tell it had happened from not having happened. One announcer now serves both encounters and medals, in two tones, with a burst at the spawn point.

**`check_life_page.mjs`'s old assertion permitted a fatal pack.** It required `count >= 2`, and 2 is exactly the size Conway kills on arrival — and it ran on `engine.clear()`, an empty board, so the crowded case was never exercised at all.

### K2. Evolution is gated on the species' REAL in-game level, which is why the cell field is `level` and not `age`

`assets/data/evolution_levels.json` is generated by `scripts/fetch_evolution_levels.py` from PokeAPI's `/evolution-chain/{id}`, keyed **by both ends of the step**: `{from_id: {to_id: min_level}}`. A per-species number could not express the corpus, because one species can evolve at different levels or not at all — Eevee carries three level-up steps (Espeon by friendship in the day, Umbreon at night, Sylveon by friendship), eight use-item steps, and four level-up steps with **no** `min_level` at all (Leafeon / Glaceon, which need a level-up move).

676 steps reported by the API collapse to **489 distinct pairs** (114 duplicate rows folded — Eevee→Leafeon appears four times). **348 pairs (71.2%) carry a real level, ranging Lv.7 to Lv.64 across 47 distinct values**; 141 (28.8%) are stones, trades, friendship or day/night.

**Three things here are traps, and each cost a wrong implementation first:**

- **Our chain ids are not PokeAPI's.** `evolution_chain_id` 429 exists here and 404s there, and its only members are Melmetal (809) and the form Melmetal-Max, so it holds no step — but "at most one chain is absent" would also pass while a second chain silently emptied, so `absent_chains` is a pinned exact set.
- **Do not resolve names through `names.en`.** For 15 records ours is not PokeAPI's: Nidoran♀/♂ collapse to one slug once the symbol is stripped, Flabébé's accents have to fold, and the Gen 8 "HP" species are deliberately misspelled in the games (Houndoor, Tailow, Minum, Skowvet) where the API has Houndoom, Talonflame, Minior, Skwovet. The id table comes from one bulk `/pokemon-species?limit=2000` GET, keyed by **PokeAPI's own spelling**, and `/pokemon?limit=1302` is the wrong source twice over: it truncates (1,351 rows exist) *and* omits the species the chain tree names as form species.
- **`/evolution-chain/{id}` is a TREE, not a line.** Eevee's eight children are leaves while Pichu's child Pikachu has its own child Raichu, so one fixed depth either drops Raichu or double-counts it.

**THE FALLBACK FOR THE 141 IS THE CORPUS MEDIAN, Lv.30 — and the alternative was measured, not argued.** The tempting per-species answer is "the level at which it learns its last level-up move" (`learnsets.json`), and it applies to all 141. Its median is **48** and its max **85** against a real median of 30, so Nidorina → Nidoqueen would have become Lv.71 and the 28.8% would run 1.6× behind the 71.2% that are real. A stated median keeps both halves on one pace and is a number the page can print; `FALLBACK_EVOLUTION_LEVEL` is exported so the HUD's tooltip cannot drift from the gate.

**FIVE PAIRS APPEAR IN BOTH HALVES, and that is the games rather than a bug.** Sandshrew → Sandslash is Lv.22 **or** an Ice Stone; Meowth → Persian is Lv.28 **or** friendship; Voltorb → Electrode is Lv.30 **or** a Leaf Stone. PokeAPI reports two steps for one pair, and the engine's answer is that the **real level wins, because a level IS one of the two routes**. `check_evolution_levels.mjs` pins those five as an exact set; the original assertion here was "no pair may overlap", which was the wrong question and would have failed on correct data.

**EVOLUTION IS NOW STRICTLY SEQUENTIAL, because the old ordering let a level-32 Bulbasaur reach Venusaur without ever being an Ivysaur.** The previous code tried stage 2 first and fell back to stage 1, which under real levels becomes a shortcut the games do not have. Walking 0 → 1 → 2 also handles the families that legitimately skip: `buildGenMetadata` puts a branching family's raw stage-3 species in `stage1Members`, so Eevee and Tyrogue still go straight from base form to far form, which is what they do.

**THE `age` FIELD WAS RENAMED TO `level` because it now IS one**, and the rename was cheap — eight sites — while a field that says `age` and holds a level would have been the kind of thing this file exists to prevent.

**THE VETERAN AURA'S GATE CHANGED WITH IT, because `age >= 5` had become true of everything.** The gold corner claims "this one has reached the level at which the ones that DO evolve would have, and never will", so it is now measured against `FALLBACK_EVOLUTION_LEVEL`.

**THE HUD LEGEND REPORTS THE MODE OF WHAT THE BOARD ACTUALLY DID**, from a new `evolvedAtLevel` field captured at the moment of evolution — read back later it would return where the cell is now, not where the gate was. An empty stage shows nothing rather than naming Lv.30 for a stage nobody on the board reached.

### K3. `backdrop-filter` turns an inert `z-index` into a real one, and life.html was the only page it happened to

`.life-toolbar` carried `position: static; z-index: 20` plus `backdrop-filter: blur(18px)`. On a static element that z-index does nothing — **but `backdrop-filter` creates a stacking context, which makes the number apply.** So the toolbar outranked `.header`'s 10, and the nav panel's `z-index: 120` is scoped *inside* that header context, so it could never beat 20.

Measured over CDP at 1440×900: with the dropdown open, **5 of 22 hit-test points along a 445px panel resolved to `.life-toolbar` / `.life-btn` / `.life-seg`**, in exactly the toolbar's band (y 55–146). The dropdown was unclickable across its top 55px. **life.html was the only page affected** — maze/sorting carry `.control-bar`, which has neither a filter nor a z-index, and maze/types/index/outfit were all measured at 0 blocked points.

The fix is one value (20 → 5). The guard is `check_life_browser.mjs`, and it is a **hit-test rather than a z-index comparison**, because both declarations are individually correct and no static check can see the interaction. It includes a **control page on the same probe**, since "zero blocked points" could equally mean a probe that finds nothing anywhere.

**Expect this shape wherever a decorative value on a non-positioned element starts meaning something** — a `filter`, a `backdrop-filter`, `transform`, `perspective`, `contain` or `will-change` will each do it, and each looks like an inert declaration in review.

### K4. The Evolution Graph Is The Edge List, Not The Stage Columns — And Three Generated Files Were Hand-Edited To Hide That

`life.html`'s branch evolution shipped as four defects at once, all of them green through the whole suite, and the fix is one architectural move: **`evolution_levels.json` is the engine's topology, not just its level numbers.**

**`stage` IN `evolution_chains.json` IS DEX ORDER, NOT DEPTH, and it cannot be anything else.** The source CSV (`data-sources/pokemon_complete_2026.csv`) has 31 columns and **no `evolves_from`**, so `extract_evolution_chains.py` numbers `stage = len(main_line_stages) + 1` over a dex-ordered list — its own comment says so. For every straight line (妙蛙種子 1 → 2 → 3) dex order IS evolution order. For a branching family it is not: chain 67 is `[133 Eevee 1, 134 3, 135 4, 136 5, 196 6, 197 7, 470 8, 471 9, 700 Sylveon 10, 10205 Eevee-Gmax 11]` — eight SIBLINGS presented as eight successive stages.

`buildGenMetadata` grouped by "the next distinct stage", so Gen 1's `stage1Members` held **水伊布 alone** and 雷/火伊布 were unreachable. Worse, **the branch's only reachable target was then evolved further**: 133 → 134 → 135 → 136, a four-generation ladder that claims 伊布 grows into 火伊布 through two intermediates. Measured over every species × stage × generation (3,075 probes), the stage-based path produced **208 evolutions with no real edge at all** — Venusaur → Ivysaur, Eevee → Vaporeon → Jolteon → Flareon. The edge-based path produces **0**.

**THE FIX READS THE EDGES THAT WERE ALREADY THERE AND ALREADY CHECKED.** `evolution_levels.json` carries `levels` (341 rows) and `unlevelled` (125) — **all 489 steps PokeAPI reports, keyed by BOTH ends**, and `check_evolution_levels.mjs` already asserts the shape of both. `unlevelled` was **loaded by `setEvolutionLevels()` and never read**; it was only reachable as the absence of a key. `setEvolutionLevels()` now builds `evolutionEdges: Map<fromId, [{to, level, trigger, detail}]>`, and `checkCellEvolution()` picks a successor from it instead of from `tree.stage1`. The stage columns still decide what SEEDS a family and what a brushed cell's stage is; they no longer decide where a cell goes. **Expect this shape whenever a table's meaning has quietly narrowed from "the graph" to "one field of it": the other field was already complete, and the reader of the narrow one never noticed.**

**THE ONE CROSS-GENERATION EXCEPTION IS A NAMED SET, and the number 70 is why.** `CROSS_GEN_BRANCH_ROOTS = {133}` lets 伊布 draw its branch from the whole dex, because 仙子伊布 (#700) is a Gen 6 species and 「大家都喜歡仙子伊布」 is not a reason to make it unreachable on the most-played board. Every other family keeps `generation === this.metadata.gen`, and **70 of the 489 edges cross a generation boundary** — so "just drop the filter" would have put #863 Annihilape (Gen 8) and #979 Palafin-Totem (Gen 9) on Gen 1 boards, and run #172 Pichu → #25 Pikachu *backwards*. The checker asserts the set is exactly `{133}` (an empty set passes "at least one exception" and removes the feature) and separately asserts, across all 9 generations, that **no species outside the set leaves its generation**. It asserts the cross-generation edge count is still 70 first, because a lock with nothing to lock out is indistinguishable from a broken one in a green run.

**THE WEIGHTS ARE THE ONLY INVENTED NUMBERS IN THE ENGINE, so their key set is pinned to the edge set.** `BRANCH_WEIGHTS` is 3/3/3 for the stones, 2/2 for friendship, 1/1/1 for location and 仙子伊布 — a design choice, not game data, since the games gate these on stones, friendship, a location and a move and the engine models none of them. They are keyed by species id and `check_life_page.mjs` asserts that key set **is exactly** 伊布's eight edge destinations, because an id absent from the map falls back to weight 1 silently. It then **measures 40,000 draws** against 3/16 and 1/16: a `weightedPick` that quietly fell through to a uniform draw would still pass a key-set check. The odds are the page's honest ones: **水/雷/火伊布 18.75% each, 太陽/月亮 12.5% each, 葉/冰/仙子 6.25% each.**

**THE MODE FREEZE IS A MODE, and the ID-only version made one of the 151 tiles unreadable.** `if (cell.speciesId === 25) return unchanged` froze Pikachu on every Gen 1 board including the classic one — and #26 雷奇's only source on a Gen 1 board is a Pikachu that evolves, so the dex could never complete with no way to see why. `MODE_FROZEN_SPECIES` is now `Map<mode, Set<id>>` and `setupGeneration(gen, starter, random, mode)` passes the modal's switch into the engine, so Let's Go freezes Pikachu and the classic board does not. Both halves are asserted, including 「no species is frozen outside a named mode」.

**THE DEX DENOMINATOR HAS TO BE THE NUMBER OF ROWS, and the roster is now ONE list.** The drawer filed 4 Eeveelutions out of the 151 and appended all 9, so it rendered **156 tiles against a `/151`** — a claim about progress the reader can check and find false. `buildDexRoster()` returns the ordered list with one optional section, the drawer renders it, and `updateHUD` divides by its length. **The section appears only where its ROOT is seeded** (Gen 1 has Eevee, Gen 4 does not), so that is one rule and not a per-generation exception list, and every one of its nine tiles is reachable on a Gen 1 board now.

**THREE OF THE FOUR FIXES WERE HAND-EDITS TO GENERATED FILES, and one of them was already RED.**

| file | generator | what the hand-edit did |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `evolution_chains.json` | `extract_evolution_chains.py` | stages 3..9 → all 2. **`check_evolution_chains.mjs` failed on the spot**: `chain 67 main_line stages are 1..n, got [1,2,2,2,2,2,2,2,2,10]` |
| `generations.json` | `extract_generations.py` | added `letsGoStarters`. Erased by the next run, and **nothing noticed** — the switch simply stopped rendering |
| `life.html` / `life.js` | — | 15 icons |

The first two were reverted; the code now reads what it needs from data that is already checked. **`letsGoStarters` moved into `extract_generations.py` as `LETS_GO_STARTERS`** because the InfoPoke snapshot has no Let's Go section at all — a constant *in the extractor* rather than a key in the JSON, and the checker asserts both halves so a regeneration cannot silently drop the mode. **Expect this shape whenever a feature cannot be derived: the fix belongs in the generator, and a key added to its output by hand has a lifetime of one regeneration.**

**`nth-of-type(2)` RESOLVES TO STEP 1, AND A SCREENSHOT WAS THE ONLY WITNESS.** The Let's Go switch was mounted with `document.querySelector('.life-modal-section-title:nth-of-type(2)')`, which matches the element that is both that class **and** the dialog's 2nd `<div>` — and `.life-modal-header` is the 1st. The control that swaps the starter list rendered inside 「步驟 1：選擇目標世代」, two sections above the cards it changes, in every screenshot, while all 145 static and 24 rendered checks passed. Step 2 now has `id="stepStarter"` and the browser checker asserts the **box**: above `#setupStarters`, below `#setupGenTabs`. **Expect this shape anywhere a selector mixes a class with a structural pseudo-class: `nth-of-type` counts TAGS among siblings, and only one sibling has the class, so the tag position and the class position are unrelated numbers.** One first-draft assertion here had the geometry comparison backwards and failed on a page that was already correct.

**ALL 26 ICONS ON THIS PAGE ARE NOW FILLED PATHS, and they were lucide STROKE paths for one commit.** `shared.css:602` gives `.icon` `fill: currentColor` and nothing else, and `stroke` defaults to `none` — so every other page on the site uses Material's filled paths, and life.html had been given lucide's, which rendered as **filled silhouettes**: the screenshot showed 「隨機種子生成」 and 「自由繪製空白網格」 as black blobs, and a checker reading text content sees the same label either way. One was `viewBox="0 0 200 200"` with a `<g fill="currentColor">`, a glyph assembled from `<circle>`s and `<path>`s that only reads correctly when stroked. Two of them also sat on a `<button>` whose `textContent` `updateHUD` overwrote, so they vanished on the first tick — hence `#btnDexCount` / `#btnHofCount` spans. **The glyph and the label must come from ONE boolean**: the play button drew a PLAY triangle beside 暫停 and PAUSE bars beside 播放, which survived because each was written next to a label rather than derived from it. The guards are a shape check (one `<path>`, 24×24 box, no `stroke=`, no `<g>`/`<rect>`/`<circle>`/`<polyline>`) **and** `check_life_browser.mjs` rasterising each glyph to a canvas and counting alpha, because the shape check cannot tell a filled path from a stroke path that merely lacks its attributes.

**`unlevelled` is the field that was always going to matter, and the cross-page consequence is the same one §3.G6 records for cry volume: state that a test cannot see.** All 8 guards above were verified by mutation, each caught by the assertion that names its own defect: the exception emptied, the weights flattened, the generation lock removed, a stroke icon returned, `LETS_GO_STARTERS` emptied, the freeze keyed by bare id, the selector put back, the counter pointed at `totalSpeciesCount`.

### K5. The Toast Card Ignored The Theme, And A Sprite On It Needed The Numbers To Decide

The wild-encounter toast's icon was 「太黑看不到」. It was not a dark icon — it was a correct icon on a card that did not belong to the theme it was rendered in, and the two facts had to be separated before either could be fixed.

**THE CARD'S BACKGROUND WAS THE DARK THEME'S TWO TOKENS, TYPED OUT.** `linear-gradient(135deg, #1f2a4d, #111830)` is `--surface-raised` → `--bg-controls` in the dark palette, as literals. `.life-toast-icon` declared no `color` of its own, so the leaf's `fill: currentColor` inherited `--text-primary` and painted near-black ink onto navy in light theme. Measured over CDP: **1.03:1** for the icon and **2.57:1** for the eyebrow, both below WCAG's 4.5 and both invisible. The headline survived only because it hardcoded `#fff` — the one declaration that ignored the theme in the *other* direction. The fix is four tokens: the gradient, the card's `color`, `rgba(var(--accent-gold-rgb), …)` for the glow, and `var(--text-primary)` for the headline.

**THE WILD CYAN NEEDS A PER-THEME VALUE BECAUSE IT WAS A BORDER NOBODY COULD FIND.** `#7ee7ff` reads at 9.86:1 on the dark card and at **1.16:1** on the light one — and making the card themed is what created that, so the second half of the fix is the first half's consequence. Light gets `#0c8599`, measured against the **worse of the card's two gradient stops** (3.54:1) rather than against one: a card that reads 3.5:1 on one end and 2.9:1 on the other is not a readable card, and an earlier candidate `#0891b2` measured exactly 3.00 on the worst stop, which is a value that passes today and fails the moment anyone touches the gradient. `check_life_browser.mjs` takes the minimum over the stops for the same reason.

**THE SPRITE WENT ON THE CARD RATHER THAN ON A DISC, AND THE MEASUREMENT IS WHY — INCLUDING THE PART THAT SAYS THE TRADE ISN'T FREE.** All 1,025 `assets/images/sprite/` files were drawn at 40px onto both grounds and their ink compared: on the light card the median ink reads **3.98:1** and on the dark one **3.59:1**, with 11 and 17 species below 1.5:1. A disc does not remove the failure, it moves it — a light card's weak species are the **pale** ones (612 of 1,025 have their palest fifth under 1.5:1), which is exactly where the navy was strong, so the two grounds fail on disjoint sets and no single flat backing wins. 11 species on a card whose headline already states the name in text is a decorative cost worth paying; recolouring a Pokémon to fix it would not be. Same finding as §3.G8's: 「黑色系寶可夢看不見」 is the wrong diagnosis, and 捷克羅姆 at 1.01:1 is *desaturated*, not dark.

**THE ICON IS AN `<img>`, AND THAT IS THE POINT RATHER THAN A DETAIL.** An `<img>` carries its own pixels and cannot inherit a theme at all, so the defect class is gone rather than mitigated. `alt=""` because the headline carries the name as text one element away. The browser checker counts ink on five drawn sprites and requires five *different* amounts — counting the element is the check that reads like the guard, because a broken `src` is still a 44×44 box.

**THE DEX SECTION IS THE READER'S CHOICE, NOT THE GENERATION'S, and the condition is written as the FAMILY.** `buildDexRoster` takes `starterId` and adds the section only when the chosen starter's `evolution_chain_id` is a `CROSS_GEN_BRANCH_ROOTS` family — today exactly 「始祖物種選了伊布」, since #133 is the only one of Gen 1's starters inside chain 67. Writing it as the id would need a second branch the moment a root exists; writing it as a per-generation list is the shape §3.K4 just removed. With any other starter the roster is the generation's own list again and the counter returns to `/151` **because it is the same list** — no second condition to keep in sync. The browser check drives the real modal for both halves, because 伊布 is only reachable through the Let's Go switch: a check that merely looked for a `#133` card would be asserting about a list that does not exist in classic mode.

**THE ENCOUNTER CRY IS OFF BY DEFAULT, SILENT ABOVE 2×, AND NEVER AWAITED — three decisions and three different reasons.**

- **Off by default.** Measured over 20 × 60s per generation: a wild encounter fires every **2.0s at 10×**, 3.8s at 5×, 11.5s at 2×. Cries are 1.10s at the median, 3.26s at the worst, so at 10× a cry is sounding more than half the time. The default is `cryPlayer.muteForView(true)` and **never** `cryPlayer.muted = true`, which is §3.G6's bug verbatim: the setter persists to the site-wide key, so merely loading this page would silence `cries.html`, `detail.html` and `spectrum.html`.
- **Silent above 2×**, and the toast still fires. The speed control exists to watch population change, and at 5×/10× the board is a blur nobody reads an arrival off. `CRY_SILENT_ABOVE_SPEED = 2` is checked against the speed table's own `case 2:` slot, because a ceiling nothing measures is a preference.
- **Never awaited.** `cry-player.js:297` is `if (state === 'suspended') await ctx.resume()` with **no timeout and no catch** — measured, a headless probe past 120s. Until the reader has clicked something that promise does not settle, so `await cryPlayer.play(...)` in the encounter path would suspend the handler for the life of the page. It is fire-and-forget with `.catch(() => {})`, and the docblock says why rather than tidying it as `.catch`.

**TWO GUARDS WERE WRITTEN WRONG FIRST, AND BOTH FAILURES ARE THE SAME SHAPE AS §3.G'S.** The site-wide-mute and cry-path checks matched `cryPlayer.muted = true` and `assets/cries/` **in the comments that explain the bug** — the fourth time this file has been caught matching prose, so `lifeJsCode` (comments stripped) is now what every source assertion reads. And a mutation that swapped the muted guard with the speed guard passed every assertion, which is correct: the swap is behaviour-preserving, and the mutation that *is* a defect (moving the muted guard down past `has()`) is now caught by an index comparison. **The gap guard had no assertion at all**, and a guard that would only have checked `CRY_MIN_GAP_MS` still exists would have passed a page with no throttle — it is now asserted as applied, and asserted as testing the gap **before** stamping `lastEncounterCryAt`, because stamping first turns "this long between cries" into "this long between attempts" and silently halves the rate at 2× with nothing on screen to say so.

**The browser checker takes the toast's foreground/background contrast from the RENDERED page in both themes, which is the only place 1.03:1 exists.** It deliberately does *not* assert that the computed background contains no navy hex: in dark theme `--surface-raised` resolves to `rgb(31, 42, 77)`, so such a check cannot tell a token from a typed-in value. 「The two themes differ」 is the falsifiable claim; 「the stylesheet names no literal」 belongs to the static half. That is 61 browser checks, up from 38, and 13 of the new guards above were verified by mutation.
